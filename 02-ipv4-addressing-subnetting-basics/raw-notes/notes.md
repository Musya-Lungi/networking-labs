# Day 2 Learning Notes — IPv4 Addressing & Subnetting

## What I Learned
- IPv4 addresses are 32-bit numbers represented in dotted decimal format
- Each octet represents 8 bits (0–255 range)
- Binary conversion is essential for understanding subnetting logic

---

## Key Confusions

- Converting decimal to binary is still slow under pressure
- Struggling to quickly identify network vs host portion
- CIDR notation (/24, /16, etc.) needs more repetition

---

## Mistakes Made

- Initially mixed up subnet mask purpose with IP address
- Forgot that same subnet means direct communication without router
- Misread binary weights (128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1)

---

## Insights

- Subnetting is not math — it is network segmentation logic
- Repetition is more important than memorization
- Real understanding comes from packet flow thinking, not formulas alone

---

## Improvement Plan

- Practice binary conversion daily for speed
- Do at least 10 subnetting problems per day
- Visualize network flow instead of memorizing rules

---

## Key Takeaway

Subnetting becomes intuitive only after repeated exposure to network flow patterns — not theory alone.
